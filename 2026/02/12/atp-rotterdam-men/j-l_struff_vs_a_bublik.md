### Variance Drivers

- **Three-set probability (48.8%):** Nearly a coin-flip between straight sets (20-24 games) and three sets (26-33 games), creating significant total variance. A straight-sets outcome severely undercuts the Over 22.5 bet.

- **Tiebreak uncertainty (28.4% P(≥1 TB)):** Modest tiebreak sample sizes (Struff: 9 TBs, Bublik: 14 TBs) make tiebreak probability estimates less reliable. Each tiebreak adds ~2 games, materially impacting the total. If one or two tiebreaks occur, Over 22.5 becomes highly likely; if none occur, Under becomes probable.

- **Elo-form divergence:** Struff's +56 Elo advantage conflicts with Bublik's superior game win percentage, hold rate, and recent form. This creates spread volatility: if Struff's historical quality asserts itself, he could keep the match close or win outright. If Bublik's current form dominates, the spread could balloon to -5 or more.

- **Set closure efficiency:** Struff's 94.3% serving-for-set rate vs Bublik's 87.6% creates asymmetric risk. If Struff wins the first set, his high closure rate makes a Struff 2-0 outcome more likely than if Bublik wins the first set (where his lower closure rate gives Struff more breakback opportunities). This asymmetry widens the spread CI.

- **First-time matchup:** No H2H history means no empirical validation of the matchup dynamics. Stylistic factors (e.g., does Struff's game particularly trouble Bublik's serve, or vice versa?) are unknown, introducing uncertainty not captured in aggregate statistics.

### Data Limitations

- **Surface specification:** Briefing lists surface as "all" rather than "hard", requiring assumption that Rotterdam is played on hard courts and that hard court stats are most relevant. If the surface assumption is wrong, hold/break rates could be miscalibrated.

- **Tiebreak sample sizes:** Struff (9 TBs in 60 matches) and Bublik (14 TBs in 78 matches) provide limited tiebreak samples, reducing confidence in tiebreak win rate estimates (55.6% and 57.1% respectively). Wider error bars on TB probability propagate to total games variance.

- **No H2H data:** Zero head-to-head matches means the model relies purely on individual statistics without matchup-specific validation. First-time matchups carry hidden risk from unknown stylistic interactions.
